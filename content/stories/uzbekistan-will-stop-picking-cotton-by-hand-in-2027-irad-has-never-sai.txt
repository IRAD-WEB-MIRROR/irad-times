Uzbekistan's agriculture minister said on 9 November that the country will switch entirely to mechanised cotton harvesting from 2027. By his account about 75 to 80 per cent of this year's Uzbek crop was picked by machine, two regions reached full mechanisation, and about 53,000 hectares of cotton land will be moved to other crops.

In Irad the announcement was treated as foreign news. It is also a measuring stick laid along the ISEZ's own frontier. Irad's cotton is grown on the Fergana-rim farm belt in Lower Naryn District, within sight of the Uzbek fields near Bazar-Korgon, and ginned in Chu District. The country has just closed a record picking season of 41,000 tonnes, already reported. What has not been reported is how that crop was picked, by whom, for how much, and with how much water.

The Times put those questions to the Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources in writing on Monday. The ministry did not reply by the time this paper went to press. The Times does not suggest that any law has been broken. It reports that the figure Tashkent has just published for itself has no Irad counterpart.

## What the papers show

The Territorial Statistics Office publishes production figures. The Times found no published table that separates machine picking from hand picking, no count of cotton hectares by village, and no figure for the water a cotton field takes in Lower Naryn. The ministry's provisional irrigation schedule, issued by 15 April each year since the Chu farmers' petition of 2019, lists canals and rotation days. It does not state volumes per hectare.

The one paper that touches both countries' cotton is the February 2025 memorandum on trade and border coordination between the ISEZ and Uzbekistan. It is a memorandum, not a treaty. It created a joint working group on livestock and cotton shipments, which first met at Tash-Kömür in April 2025. Its minutes have not been published. Xinjiang Lihua Co., which holds ginning concessions in the ISEZ, did not reply to questions about how much of the rim crop reaches its gins on machine-picked terms.

## What they say

Dilfuza Rakhimova, 38, has picked on a farm near Korgoncho for nine autumns. She is paid by weight at the field edge, 0.35 som a kilo, and on a good day carries in about 55 kilos. That is a little under 20 som, less than a plate of plov at 28. 'Women and students pick. The men drive the trailers,' she said. She had not heard of the Uzbek plan.

Anvar Yusupov, 52, grows cotton and wheat on a walled Korgoncho farmstead. He said the farmstead's small fields, the canals and the old walls leave no room to turn a machine, and that the few harvesters he has seen work on the larger farms. 'A machine wants a long field. Here we have short ones,' he said. Water decides more than labour, he added: the rota on the Naryn-fed canals fixes how many times a field is watered, and a late turn costs more than a missing picker. The Lower Naryn Irrigation Section runs those canals for the ministry.

Toktosun Abdraev, 65, farms potatoes near Nurzhan, where the gin trucks come in. He sees the other side of the labour question. 'In October everyone wants hands at once, for cotton, for potatoes, for apples. Storage first, prices later,' he said. Mairam Turdueva, 54, who keeps sheep and hay in the same oasis, said her neighbours' hay water comes off the same canal system and that nobody has ever told them what a hectare of anything is allowed.

## Why it matters

Uzbekistan's reason for ending hand picking is a policy choice made in public, with a date and a hectare count. Irad's rim farmers do not know whether to expect the same from Talas, or whether the lower labour cost of the rim's pickers is now the sole argument for keeping the crop. If Uzbekistan moves 53,000 hectares out of cotton, it will also free irrigation water in the same basin that feeds the Naryn canals, and the Toktogul schedule under the Naryn Protocol, next renewed in 2027, is where that would be settled.

The ministry can answer three questions without a new study. What share of the 2026 crop was picked by machine? What is the water allowance per hectare of cotton on each rim canal? And will the joint working group's minutes be published before the 2027 season is sown? Until it does, a farmer in Korgoncho will learn the rim's plan from the Uzbek papers.
